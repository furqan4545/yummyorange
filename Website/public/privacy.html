Privacy policy

Last changed 28 November 2026

YummyOrange is made by Epoxlab LLC. The app works on your files where they are, on your Mac, and doesn’t collect, send or sell anything about you or your files. This website uses analytics to learn how people use it; that part is described below.

The app

  • Your files stay on your Mac. YummyOrange reads a file in its own folder and writes the result beside it. Nothing is uploaded, and document importers never fetch anything from the network.
  • Network requests. The app makes two kinds of request of its own, both to this website (yummyorange.com): update checks and, on a Mac without ffmpeg, the ffmpeg download.
  • Update checks. The app asks whether a new version of YummyOrange is out (the update information and its signature), about once an hour, and downloads it when you choose to install it. When an update is installed, macOS also checks the new version’s signing certificate with Apple, as it does for any signed app. Settings › General can turn the automatic check off.
  • The ffmpeg download. On a Mac without ffmpeg, YummyOrange downloads its own copy (about 12 MB, the same file for everyone) once: about a minute after it opens, on a network that isn’t Low Data Mode or a phone’s hotspot, or as soon as you start on a sound or video. If that fails, the next automatic try waits longer each time, up to three days; starting on a sound or video, or Download in Settings › About, tries at once. Once it’s installed, YummyOrange asks again only when an update comes with a new build of it. A Mac that already has a working ffmpeg never makes this request. YummyOrange’s ffmpeg is built without network code: it only reads and writes files. Its license and source are on the FFmpeg page.
  • What the requests carry. Both name the app and its version; their other headers are the same for every copy (the language is always English), and they carry no identifier, no cookies and nothing about your files. Like any request they reach the website’s host from your address (see below).
  • No account and no analytics. There is nothing to sign up for, and the app has no analytics, crash reporting or advertising code.
  • What it keeps. Your settings, stored by macOS for YummyOrange on your Mac. The Clothesline keeps a list of the files hung on it (it only points to them), and copied screenshots you choose to hang are saved in Pictures › Clothesline. On a Mac without ffmpeg, YummyOrange’s own ffmpeg is kept in ~/Library/Application Support/YummyOrange. Nothing of this leaves your Mac.
  • When you send something. AirDrop and Move To do what you ask with the files you pick, through macOS.
  • Permissions. macOS asks before YummyOrange first saves into your Desktop, Documents or Downloads folder or onto an external drive, and before it reads the first copied screenshot. Accessibility access is needed only for the optional Hold the key first. Update notices arrive quietly in Notification Center, without a permission prompt; Settings › Notifications can turn them off or into banners.

This website

  • Analytics. The site uses PostHog to learn how it is used: which pages are opened, where people click and scroll, how fast pages load, script errors, and recordings of visits (session replays) that show the pointer and the page as you saw it. Text typed into any field is masked and never recorded. PostHog keeps a random ID in your browser’s storage so that visits from the same browser can be told apart; it isn’t linked to your name, email or anything you have on your Mac. The data is stored by PostHog in the United States and used only to improve this site, never sold or used for advertising.
  • Apart from PostHog’s script, everything on the site (type, pictures and scripts) comes from this site.
  • The try-it demos run in your browser: nothing you drag is uploaded, and the files and links in them are made up. How you use them is part of the analytics above.
  • Like any web server, the one that hosts these pages may keep short-lived access logs (the address a request came from, the time and the page). They are used only to keep the site running.
